@abstract
Searching for a missing person across many Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) cameras is a natural application of face recognition, but it creates a serious privacy problem. Pooling footage or face data from many sites in one place exposes every bystander who walked past a camera and creates a single point of failure. This project, FedMPS (Federated Missing Person Search), builds and evaluates a missing-person search system in which camera sites collaborate without sharing raw video, face crops or unprotected biometric templates. A pretrained ArcFace face-recognition model of 43.6 million parameters is kept frozen, and only a small residual adapter is trained by Federated Learning (FL) with FedProx (Federated Proximal) across five simulated camera nodes. Each model update is 1.05 MB instead of 174.4 MB. Three privacy mechanisms are implemented and measured: Secure Aggregation (SA) with pairwise Diffie–Hellman masks, Differential Privacy (DP) on model updates, and cancelable watchlist templates that are keyed differently for every site. An operational pipeline performs face detection, tracking, quality gating, matching and track-level voting at each node. A human operator must confirm every alert before any face crop reaches the server, and every action is recorded in an audit log.

The final evaluation uses all 628,699 images of 4,605 identities in VGGFace2-HQ, degraded to CCTV quality. Following the supervisor's protocol, 70% of the identities train across the five nodes and the other 30% are held out as the validation set, giving a 691-person watchlist and 31,850 probes of people who are not on it. The training settings were chosen in a pilot on training identities only, and federated training ran for 200 rounds. Federated training matches centralised training on pooled data, with a rank-1 accuracy of 0.9734 against 0.9733, and beats training at each site alone by 1.1 rank-1 points. Against the frozen backbone, rank-1 stays at its ceiling of 0.973, while the True Positive Identification Rate (TPIR) at a 1% False Positive Identification Rate (FPIR) rises from 0.9054 to 0.9107, a small but consistent gain in open-set search. SA costs no accuracy, a corrected membership-inference attack finds no leakage with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.501, 512-bit templates cost 4.7 rank-1 points, and DP has no operating point that is both private and usable. On real CCTV video from the ChokePoint dataset, the live pipeline raised a correct alert for 95.2% of walk-throughs by enrolled people, typically within 1.3 seconds, and never named the wrong person. These results, including the negative ones, are reported as measured.

**Keywords:** Federated learning, face recognition, missing person search, secure aggregation, differential privacy, cancelable biometrics, open-set identification.
