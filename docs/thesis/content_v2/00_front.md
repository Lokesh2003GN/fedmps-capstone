@abstract
Searching for a missing person across many CCTV cameras was a natural application of face recognition, but it created a serious privacy problem, because pooling footage or face data from many sites in one place exposed every bystander who walked past a camera and created a single point of failure. This project, FedMPS, built and evaluated a missing-person search system in which camera sites collaborated without sharing raw video, face crops or unprotected biometric templates. A pretrained ArcFace face-recognition model of 43.6 million parameters was kept frozen, and only a small residual adapter of 263,424 parameters was trained by FL with FedProx across five simulated camera nodes, so that each model update was 1.05 MB instead of 174.4 MB. Three privacy mechanisms were implemented and measured, namely SA with pairwise Diffie–Hellman masks, DP on model updates and cancelable watchlist templates keyed differently for every site. An operational pipeline performed face detection, tracking, quality gating, matching and track-level voting at each node, a human operator confirmed every alert before any face crop reached the server, and every action was recorded in an audit log.

The final evaluation used all 628,699 images of the 4,605 identities in VGGFace2-HQ, degraded to CCTV quality. Seventy percent of the identities trained across the five nodes and the other thirty percent were held out as the validation set, which gave a 691-person watchlist and 31,850 probes of people who were not on it. The training settings were chosen in a pilot on training identities only, and federated training ran for 200 rounds. Federated training matched centralised training on pooled data with a rank-1 accuracy of 0.9734 against 0.9733 and beat training at each site alone by 1.1 rank-1 points. Against the frozen backbone, rank-1 stayed at its ceiling of 0.973 while the TPIR at a 1% FPIR rose from 0.9054 to 0.9107, a small but consistent gain in open-set search. SA cost no accuracy, a corrected MIA found no leakage with an AUC of 0.501, 512-bit templates cost 4.7 rank-1 points, and DP had no operating point that was both private and usable. On real CCTV faces from the ChokePoint dataset, the trained system found and correctly named almost every enrolled person, and the complete case workflow was demonstrated with the consoles on a recorded university event video. These results, including the negative ones, were reported as measured.

**Keywords:** FL, face recognition, missing person search, SA, DP, cancelable biometrics, open-set identification, CCTV
